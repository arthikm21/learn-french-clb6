Reading format at a glance

TCF Canada Compréhension écrite contains 39 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes. The difficulty rises across the section, from familiar notices and messages to longer texts, opinions, and abstract arguments. The official result uses a 0–699 scale; IRCC currently maps 453–498 to NCLC 7.

There is no benefit in spending equal time on every item. Protect enough time for the later passages while banking the shorter early questions accurately.

Read the document before chasing details

For each text, identify four things first: who wrote it, who it is for, why it exists, and what action or conclusion it supports. This prevents a common mistake: choosing an option that repeats a true detail but misses the author's purpose.

  • Notices and advertisements: locate conditions, exclusions, dates, and required action.
  • Emails and letters: track relationship, register, problem, and next step.
  • News and informational texts: separate the main claim from supporting examples.
  • Opinion texts: map position, concession, contrast, cause, and consequence.

How to review a wrong reading answer

  1. Underline the smallest passage that proves the correct answer.
  2. Name the distractor: copied phrase, reversed meaning, true-but-irrelevant detail, overstatement, or unsupported inference.
  3. Rewrite the question in simpler French or English.
  4. Save one reusable phrase or connector from the passage.
  5. Re-answer the item two days later without looking at the key.
