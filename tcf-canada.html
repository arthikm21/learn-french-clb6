TCF Canada in one minute

The Test de connaissance du français pour le Canada is an official French test accepted by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) for specified immigration and citizenship applications. The immigration version has four mandatory tests. France Éducation international reports a result for each ability; IRCC then maps each result to an NCLC level.

NCLC is the French scale. CLB is the parallel English scale. People often search for “CLB 7 French,” but the official French-language term is NCLC 7.

The four tests total approximately 2 hours 47 minutes. Listening and reading become progressively harder. Speaking is face-to-face with an examiner; speaking and writing are assessed by trained raters.

Choose the right target before you study

Do not prepare toward a vague idea of “passing.” TCF Canada has no single pass mark: your required NCLC level depends on the immigration or citizenship pathway. For many Express Entry candidates, NCLC 7 in all four abilities is an important threshold, but it is not the requirement for every program.

Use the TCF Canada score chart to turn your target NCLC level into four concrete score ranges. A strong ability does not compensate for another ability below a program's minimum.

A preparation loop that produces useful evidence

  1. Diagnose: take one timed listening and reading set; record one speaking response and write one timed task.
  2. Classify errors: separate language gaps from timing, misunderstood instructions, distractors, and incomplete task delivery.
  3. Train the weakest ability: spend half of each week on the lowest result and split the rest across the other three.
  4. Re-test: repeat a comparable task weekly and track raw accuracy, completion, and recurring errors.
  5. Calibrate: use official samples for format and qualified human feedback for speaking and writing.
